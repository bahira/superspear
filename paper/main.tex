\begin{abstract}
We present SPEAR, a symbolic regression engine that discovers closed-form mathematical laws from data via multi-objective genetic programming, then compiles every discovery to three execution backends---JavaScript/WebAssembly, strict MISRA-C:2012 C99, and PyTorch---under machine-checked numeric-parity audits. Across a 48+-task registry spanning neural-network activations, shader primitives, special functions, physics laws, and control policies, SPEAR produced nine exact solutions at machine precision, eighteen kernels cheaper than their exact references in an ALU/SFU cost model, and seven replacements of iterative numerical solvers, with speedups up to $\times1840$. We contribute a taxonomy of four difficulty regimes for symbolic search, a scaffold-seeding methodology validated on adversarial optimality tests, dual precise/fast operating points per law, and honest failure cases: an expert hand-fit (Narkowicz ACES) resists evolution at any tested depth, and a discovered GELU that is $\times2.32$ faster as raw WebAssembly loses accuracy and throughput inside PyTorch.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Production software re-implements the same small mathematical kernels millions of times per second: activation functions, interpolation curves, color transfers, wave evaluators, normalization factors. When these kernels rely on transcendental functions ($\exp$, $\mathrm{erf}$, $\arccos$), their cost explodes on \emph{transcendental-poor} hardware---microcontrollers without SFUs, low-end GLSL profiles, quantized pipelines where $\exp$ is many times the price of a multiply. Hand-derived algebraic replacements exist for a lucky few (fast inverse square root~\cite{lomont2003invsqrt}, Pad\'e approximants, the handbook fits of Abramowitz and Stegun~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}, the Acklam normal quantile~\cite{acklam2003invnorm}); deriving new ones is slow expert work.

SPEAR automates both halves of this problem. First, the \emph{derivation}: a multi-objective genetic program searches for closed-form expressions that trade accuracy against ALU/SFU cost, so cheapness is an objective rather than an afterthought. Second, the \emph{verification burden} that normally blocks adoption: every discovered formula ships with bit-faithful JavaScript/WebAssembly and MISRA-C:2012 C99 implementations proven numerically equivalent to the evaluator's semantics, plus a PyTorch emission.

A clarifying negative result frames scope early: for LLM inference we probed whether symbolic regression can find ``a formula for matmul.'' It cannot, because none is needed---a generic dot product has tensor rank $n$, so $4m+3a$ units is provably unbeatable per output lane, and there is nothing iterative to replace. What \emph{can} be attacked is everything glued around the GEMV, and everything else programs recompute forever.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Symbolic regression engines.} Evolutionary symbolic regression is mature; NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2} supplies the multi-objective backbone used here. PySR~\cite{cranmer2020pysr,cranmer2023pysr} is the reference high-performance engine; SymbolicGPT~\cite{valipour2021symbolicgpt} explores transformer-based SR. These systems target discovery quality; none ships, as its core artifact, a parity-audited deployment pipeline across JS/WASM/C backends. We do not claim search superiority over PySR-class engines.

\textbf{Hand-crafted fast kernels.} The fast inverse square root~\cite{lomont2003invsqrt}, the Acklam inverse-normal rational fit~\cite{acklam2003invnorm}, handbook approximations~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}, blackbody RGB fits~\cite{helland2012blackbody}, and the Narkowicz ACES tonemap fit~\cite{narkowicz2015aces} are expert one-offs. Our results are complementary: evolution rediscovers several such forms (e.g.\ the atan Pad\'e $[3/2]$) and honestly loses to others (\S\ref{sec:regimes}).

\textbf{Efficient attention.} KV-cache eviction policies such as H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o}, StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm}, and SnapKV~\cite{li2024snapkv} motivate our decision-rule tasks; transformer attention~\cite{vaswani2017attention} and the Distil recipe~\cite{sanh2019distilbert} underpin the real-model validation of \S\ref{sec:realmodels}.

The claimed novelty is therefore not ``evolution finds formulas,'' but the combination of: (i) a \emph{parity-audited deployment pipeline} (MISRA lint, real gcc compile, C/WASM/JS numeric parity, permanent CI replay); (ii) \emph{dual operating points} per law (precise/fast); (iii) an \emph{ALU/SFU cost model inside the search loop}; (iv) a \emph{four-regime difficulty taxonomy}; and (v) a \emph{scaffold-seeding methodology} validated on adversarial optimality tests.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Search}
Multi-objective NSGA-II~\cite{deb2002nsga2} evolves expression trees; objectives are accuracy (MSE on a train split) and formula cost. Operators are $\texttt{add}, \texttt{sub}, \texttt{mul}, \texttt{pdiv}, \texttt{relu}, \texttt{abs}, \texttt{neg}, \texttt{sq}, \texttt{sqrt}, \texttt{cube}, \texttt{max}, \texttt{min}, \texttt{exp}, \texttt{sin}, \texttt{cos}, \texttt{log}, \texttt{atan}$. Transcendentals are allowed but priced honestly, so the Pareto front itself encodes the accuracy/cost trade-off. Regression milestones are calibrated against the measured noise floor of each dataset rather than absolute thresholds; budget is allocated across tasks by UCB (exploit what improves, explore what stagnates); end-to-end seeded RNG makes every record replayable from one integer.

\subsection{Cost model}
Cost is counted in GPU-style ALU/SFU units: $\texttt{mul}/\texttt{add}=1$, $\texttt{div}=4$, $\texttt{sqrt}=2$, transcendental ($\exp/\sin/\cos/\log$) ${\approx}\,20$. Iterative baselines are billed the same way---every solver iteration counts---which is what legitimizes the large speedups of \S\ref{sec:iterative}. The model is cross-checked by wall-clock benchmarks on WASM-compiled pairs (formula vs.\ exact law).

\subsection{Protected division rails}
The engine's protected division (\texttt{pdiv}) clamps denominators below $10^{-4}$ and outputs beyond $\pm 10^{4}$. Codegen reproduces these rails bit-faithfully on all three backends, so parity holds even near saturation. Notably, the Gaussian-CDF champion \emph{exploits the rails as free saturation}: its tiny divisor turns the division into a hard plateau, exactly the shape of $\Phi(x)$; the algebraic strength-reduction (division by constant $\to$ multiplication by reciprocal, $-3$ units per site) therefore only rewrites divisors above the protection floor, and each rewrite passes a metric-parity gate before entering the ledger.

\subsection{Verification pipeline}
Every champion passes four gates: (a) static MISRA-C:2012 lint (zero dynamic allocation, branchless saturations via \texttt{fminf}/\texttt{fmaxf}, fixed-width types, no loops, no \texttt{powf}); (b) real compilation with \texttt{gcc -std=c99 -Wall -Wextra -pedantic -O2}; (c) numeric parity C\,$\leftrightarrow$\,WASM\,$\leftrightarrow$\,JS within $10^{-4}$ relative (latest full audit: ${\leq}\,10^{-6}$), enforced by the rail-faithful codegen above; (d) wall-clock benchmark against the exact reference law, both compiled to WebAssembly over 200k evaluations. A permanent CI job replays the parity sweep across all ledger champions on every push. The audit has already caught a latent bug (multi-variable WASM modules silently returning NaN from a wrapper ABI mismatch).

\subsection{Deployment shape: dual slots}
Each task stores two validated forms: the \emph{precise} champion (lowest error ever found) and a \emph{fast} variant (cheapest form passing level-2 validation). Nineteen of twenty-seven ledger tasks have a genuinely cheaper second form; displaced champions are demoted, never lost, and any fast variant no longer actually cheaper than its champion is pruned. Largest fast-slot cost cuts: Lambert W$_0$ $22{\to}1$ units ($\times22$), Gaussian kernel $24{\to}1$ ($\times24$), Hill dose--response $\times9$, Gaussian CDF $29{\to}6$ ($\times4.8$), Softplus $\times4.5$, Sigmoid $27{\to}7$ ($\times3.9$); the Kerr-with-spin fast slot reaches $\times480$ vs.\ RK4.

\subsection{Scaffold seeding}
Discovery quality depends less on budget than on which algebraic bricks are available at warm-up. Warm-up pools provide primitive shapes (rationals, Pad\'e $[3/2]$, exponential decay, reciprocal powers, the rsqrt family $c/\sqrt{c_2 \pm d x^2}$); champion ASTs are re-injected as warm-up bricks for \emph{other} tasks (variable-renamed, size-capped, never the task's own answer). Adding reciprocal powers plus rsqrt seeds produced the largest single-run jump of the project: Kerr weak-field MSE $5.4\times10^{-5} \to 1.4\times10^{-7}$ ($\sim$380$\times$). Seeding the \emph{shape} of Huber's max-trick (without constants) converted a stuck candidate into a solution; acos$\to$atan identity scaffolds cracked two tasks whose truths require an unserved transcendental.

\subsection{Real-model validation on distilgpt2}
\label{sec:realmodels}
Simulated benchmarks were resolved against a real model before claiming anything.

\textbf{KV-cache eviction (positive, narrow).} On real distilgpt2 attention traces (72 layer-prompt samples, keep 40), the evolved 5-unit policy $4.5\,S + A + R$ (Sinks + accumulated Attention + Recency, discovered in 4 iterations on simulated data) retains \textbf{80.31\%} of future attention mass on average, vs.\ \textbf{80.20\%} for H2O~\cite{zhang2023h2o}, \textbf{80.07\%} for StreamingLLM~\cite{xiao2024streamingllm} (SnapKV~\cite{li2024snapkv} family policies occupy the same design space; random eviction: 73.82\%; oracle upper bound: \textbf{90.85\%}). The margin is narrow but positive, achieved with 3 features and no attention-score sorting.

\textbf{GELU swap (negative, kept).} Swapping distilgpt2's fused GELU for the discovered algebraic form (the $\times6.57$/$\times2.32$ winner above) \textbf{raises perplexity by 0.56} (5.89 $\to$ 6.45) and \textbf{lowers throughput by 9.2\%} (362 $\to$ 329 tok/s) on CPU. Native fused kernels beat multi-op algebraics inside frameworks; the cost-model gains transfer only where transcendentals are truly expensive (raw WASM: $\times2.12$ measured; MCUs). Frameworks are the wrong battlefield --- this is a deployment-boundary result, not a search failure.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Bessel depth.} Bessel-family tasks ($J_0/J_1$) resist current depths: series scaffolds mutate away before compression. One earlier wall was a poisoned reference (wrong series); after fixing the reference, $J_0$ improved $\times60$.
\item \textbf{Simulated-vs-real nuance, now resolved.} Early KV-cache numbers were simulated; validation on real distilgpt2 traces (\S\ref{sec:realmodels}) confirms a narrow positive margin --- and the GELU swap confirms that framework deployment can erase raw-kernel gains entirely.
\item \textbf{Single-machine benchmarks.} Edge timings come from one CPU-only machine. The cost model counts ALU/SFU units; wall-clock gains on SFU-rich GPUs will be smaller than modeled.
\item \textbf{Expert hand-fits remain strong.} One expert hand-fit (Narkowicz ACES) resisted every budget tried. We claim complementarity with, not superiority over, expert derivation.
\item \textbf{Benchmark-domain records.} Records are measured on benchmark domains; forms fitted on a bounded interval can diverge outside it (flagged in the ledger).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reproducibility}
Every record carries its seed, iteration count, serialized AST, cost breakdown, and both operating points; same seed $\Rightarrow$ same data, same formulas, same metrics. \texttt{npm ci \&\& npx tsx scripts/test-wasm-parity.ts} replays the full parity audit; \texttt{npx tsx scripts/run-farm.ts <seed> <budget> <workers>} replays discovery. MIT licensed.

\section{Conclusion}
SPEAR treats kernel replacement as a full pipeline, not just a search: an ALU/SFU-priced NSGA-II finds formulas; rails-faithful codegen and MISRA/gcc/parity audits make them deployable; dual precise/fast slots expose the accuracy-latency trade-off deployers actually live by; and a four-regime taxonomy plus scaffold-seeding methodology tell practitioners where to spend iterations. The honest outcomes matter as much as the wins: evolution rediscovers classic human forms, loses gracefully to excellent ones, and its framework-level gains evaporate exactly where fused native kernels are already cheap. Future work: deeper Bessel-capable scaffolds, GPU wall-clock validation, and richer seed grammars for regime-R4 tricks.
